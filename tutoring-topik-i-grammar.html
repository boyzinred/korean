Batchim (Consonant Endings) Guide

What is Batchim?

Batchim (받침) refers to the final consonant (or consonant cluster) at the bottom of a Korean syllable. For example, in 학 (hag), the ㄱ is the batchim. Many Korean grammar rules change depending on whether a noun or verb stem ends in a batchim (consonant) or a vowel.

Particles (Nouns)

Nouns ending in a consonant (batchim) typically take particles starting with a vowel to make pronunciation easier:
• Topic: 은 (학생은)
• Subject: 이 (학생이)
• Object: 을 (학생을)

Nouns ending in a vowel take particles starting with a consonant:
• Topic: 는 (학교는)
• Subject: 가 (학교가)
• Object: 를 (학교를)

Verb/Adjective Endings

When attaching grammar patterns starting with -(으) (like -(으)세요 or -(으)면), you must check the stem:
• Consonant ending: Add the '으' (e.g., 먹다 → 먹 + 으면 = 먹으면).
• Vowel ending: Skip the '으' (e.g., 가다 → 가 + 면 = 가면).
• ㄹ Batchim Exception: Stems ending in ㄹ act like vowels for these patterns (e.g., 만들다 → 만들면).
