The Word Bank — All 1358, by Part of Speech

Days 1–27 — Learn

Every study day is two separate runs of Exercise 2. Do not put them in one selection: mixing 50 unfamiliar words into 200 familiar ones hides how the new batch actually went.

Run 1, the new batch. Tick today's day only. Question language Korean, answer English. Leave “Retry a word until it is right” on. 50 words.

Run 2, the review. Tick four boxes — the days 1, 3, 7 and 16 back — and leave today's unticked. Question language English, answer Korean, because recall is the direction that fails first. Set Word limit to 60 so a ~200 word pool becomes a random 60, and put 15 in Timer in minutes.

On day 20 that is: run 1 = day 20; run 2 = days 19, 17, 13 and 4. Early on there is nothing 16 back, so tick only the boxes that exist.

Days 28–41 — Consolidate

No new words — the bank stops at day 27. This fortnight is two full passes over all 1358, then the leftovers. Use Check range for the blocks.

Days 28–32, pass one. Korean → English. One block a day: days 1–6, 7–11, 12–17, 18–22, 23–27. About 270 words each, 25 minutes on the timer.

Days 33–37, pass two. Same five blocks, but English → Korean. Harder, and the direction the writing paper needs.

Days 38–40, the leftovers. Every run ends with a Words to review box holding what you missed. Press Send to paste practice and it carries them to the Paste Your Own List page, where you drill just those until a run comes back clean.

Day 41, rehearsal. Tick All days, Word limit 100, timer 20, Korean → English. A random hundred out of the whole bank is a fair read on where you actually stand.

Two weeks is the shortest tail worth running: it is what gives the words you met on day 27 a fortnight-wide gap before the exam. Ending at day 41 leaves about two and a half weeks of slack in a two-month run.

Romanization Rules

Every Hangul character is transcribed with the strict key below. Syllables are separated by dashes, while spaces remain between separate Korean words, such as an-nyeong-ha-se-yo.

For ㄱ, ㄷ, ㄹ, and ㅂ, the first form is used at the beginning of a syllable and the second form at the end. Romanization checking removes dashes internally, so answers are accepted with or without syllable dashes.
